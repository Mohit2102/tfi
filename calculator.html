Learn more about BMR

What is BMR?

Basal metabolic rate is the least energy your body needs to keep running at rest. Breathing, circulation, cell repair and staying warm all draw on it, even if you spend the whole day in bed.

How it is calculated

The default is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which uses your age, sex, height and weight. Add your body fat % and the calculator switches to Katch-McArdle, which works from lean mass and tends to suit lean or muscular people.

Using BMR to lose weight or build muscle

Multiply BMR by an activity factor to get TDEE, the calories you burn in a day. Eat below it to lose fat, near it to hold weight, and above it to gain.

What is a good BMR?

There is no single good number. It depends on body size, age, genetics and how much lean muscle you carry. Strength training is the most practical way to raise it over time.

The formulas

Learn more about macros

What is a macro calculator?

It takes your daily calories and divides them into protein, carbohydrate and fat, each as a share of the total. The shares you choose depend on your goal, how you train and how you like to eat.

What each macro does

  • Protein repairs and builds muscle and keeps you full.
  • Carbs fuel hard training and daily energy.
  • Fat supports hormones and brain health and helps you absorb some vitamins.

How to set and adjust your macros

Start with your TDEE, then pick a split for your goal. Building muscle suits more protein with enough carbs. Endurance training suits moderate to high carbs. Fat loss suits high protein with a little less carb or fat.

Why track macros

Calories tell you how much you eat. Macros show what the food is made of, which affects fullness, training quality and how much muscle you keep while losing fat. Protein and carbs are 4 kcal per gram and fat is 9.

Learn more about body fat

What is body fat percentage?

It is the share of your total weight that is fat. The rest is lean mass: muscle, bone, organs and water.

Why composition matters more than weight

The scale cannot tell fat from muscle, and muscle is denser, so it takes up less space. Two people at the same weight can look and perform very differently.

Healthy ranges

  • Men: about 6% to 17% is typical. Athletes can sit lower. Above 25% is in the obese range.
  • Women: about 14% to 24% is typical. Women need more essential fat for hormonal and reproductive health. Above 32% is in the obese range.

How to lower body fat

Hold a steady, modest calorie deficit, keep protein high and lift with progressive overload. Crash diets cost muscle and rarely last.

Learn more about your 1RM

What is a 1RM?

Your one-rep max is the heaviest weight you can lift once with good form. This calculator estimates it from a lighter set of several reps, so you do not have to risk a true max.

Why it matters

Many programs set your weights as a percentage of 1RM. As a guide:

  • About 85% and up builds maximal strength.
  • About 65% to 80% suits muscle growth.
  • About 50% to 60% suits muscular endurance.

How to find it safely

Pick a moderate weight and do 3 to 10 reps close to failure, with good form and a spotter for heavy lifts. Enter the weight and reps here instead of attempting a true max.

How to improve it

  • Add load or reps gradually (progressive overload).
  • Refine your technique.
  • Recover with sleep, food and rest days.
  • Run strength blocks with low reps, heavy weights and longer rests.
